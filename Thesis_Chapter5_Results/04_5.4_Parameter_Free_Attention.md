## 5.4 Parameter-Free Attention

§4.3.5 fixes SimAM's marginal effect as the mean of its matched-pair differences in pooled macro F1, the same construction §5.2 and §5.3 apply to the transformer and to motion magnification. SimAM's aggregate is the smallest of the four components measured in this study, and this section's purpose is to show why that aggregate is the wrong number to read as the finding.

### 5.4.1 The effect

SimAM's mean marginal contribution is **+0.0034** pooled macro F1, computed across **four** matched pairs — positive in **three**, negative in one. Individual differences range from **−0.0288** to **+0.0341**. This is the smallest mean effect of the four components ablated in this thesis, and at this sample size it is statistically indistinguishable from zero: §4.1.5 records that every configuration was trained once, on a single seed, with no variance estimate, so a mean of +0.0034 across four pairs carries no basis for distinguishing a real small effect from noise.

### 5.4.2 The four pairs

Table 5.4 lists all four matched pairs identified in §4.3.5. SimAM is measured over four pairs rather than six because §4.1.3 rejects, as architecturally undefined, any configuration combining SimAM with no convolutional stem — SimAM re-weights activations in a convolutional feature map, and without that stem there is no feature map for it to act on. The four configurations where SimAM would pair with a stem-free model were accordingly never run. SimAM therefore has the smallest evidence base of the four components in this study.

**Table 5.4 — SimAM matched pairs.**

| Pair (off → on) | Δ pooled macro F1 |
|---|--:|
| `config_9` → `config_6` | +0.0341 |
| `config_3` → `config_5` | +0.0050 |
| `config_7` → `config_8` | +0.0034 |
| `config_13` → `config_16` | −0.0288 |

### 5.4.3 Why the mean is the wrong summary

The aggregate in §5.4.1 hides a substantial redistribution of errors across classes, and this redistribution, not the mean, is this section's real finding. Table 5.5 gives per-class F1 for the two pairs that move most: the largest gain, `config_9 → config_6`, and the largest loss, `config_13 → config_16`.

**Table 5.5 — Per-class F1 for the two most-affected SimAM pairs.**

| Configuration | Negative | Positive | Surprise |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `config_9` (SimAM off) | 0.8491 | 0.6000 | **0.3000** |
| `config_6` (SimAM on) | 0.8325 | 0.4286 | **0.5902** |
| `config_13` (SimAM off) | 0.4127 | 0.5278 | 0.4035 |
| `config_16` (SimAM on) | 0.3500 | 0.5143 | 0.3934 |

In `config_9 → config_6`, Surprise-class F1 very nearly doubles, from **0.3000** to **0.5902** — and `config_9`'s 0.3000 is the worst Surprise score of any of the twelve configurations in this study. Read in isolation, that looks like SimAM meaningfully improving the model's weakest class. But in the same pair, Positive-class F1 falls substantially, from **0.6000** to **0.4286**, and Negative-class F1 also drops slightly, from 0.8491 to 0.8325. The +0.0341 pooled macro F1 gain for this pair is the net of a large improvement on the rarest class and a substantial loss on the second-rarest one, not a uniform improvement across the board.

Both directions have to be reported together. A reader told only that SimAM nearly doubles Surprise-class F1 in this pair would take away a materially more favourable picture of the component than the data support; a reader told only that the mean effect is +0.0034 would miss that anything is happening in the per-class scores at all. Neither half of the story, alone, describes what SimAM did in this pair.

### 5.4.4 What this supports, and what it does not

§4.2 records 25 Surprise clips and 32 Positive clips in the 156-clip corpus. A change in F1 of the size seen in `config_9 → config_6` corresponds to a handful of clips moving from one predicted class to another on either side of this ledger — this is not a large-sample result on either class, and it should be read as such. The honest description of `config_9 → config_6` is that attention redistributed errors between the two minority classes in the one configuration that had handled Surprise worst of all twelve; it is not evidence that SimAM rescues the Surprise class in any general sense, since the same component's effect on `config_13 → config_16` moves Surprise-class F1 only slightly (0.4035 to 0.3934, a small decline) while the pooled macro F1 for that pair falls by the largest margin in Table 5.4, −0.0288. The two most-affected pairs in this study's SimAM comparison do not even agree on which class benefits.

### 5.4.5 Cost

SimAM adds zero learnable parameters to any configuration it appears in, confirmed in the parameter totals of §4.4.9: it re-weights an existing feature map by a closed-form energy computation, with no weight or bias learned anywhere in the operation. A component whose mean effect is statistically indistinguishable from zero and which costs nothing to include is a different proposition from a component whose mean effect is similarly small but which costs a great deal to include. The convolutional stem, treated fully in §5.5, is the second kind: §4.7.3 records that the stem alone accounts for roughly a thirteenfold increase in training time and over a hundredfold increase in peak VRAM relative to configurations without it. SimAM's near-zero mean effect is therefore not, by itself, a reason to discard the component; whether it is worth retaining turns on the cost it is weighed against, and for SimAM that cost is zero.
