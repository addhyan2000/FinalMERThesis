## 5.3 Motion Magnification

§4.3.5 fixes magnification's marginal effect as the mean of six matched-pair differences in pooled macro F1, the same construction §5.2 applies to the transformer. This section reports that effect, the six pairs it is computed from, and why the near-zero mean is the least informative number this section can offer.

### 5.3.1 The effect

Motion magnification's mean marginal contribution across its six matched pairs is **+0.0152** pooled macro F1 — positive in **four** of the six pairs, negative in two. Individual differences range from **−0.0541** to **+0.0795**. Unlike the transformer's six-of-six result in §5.2.1, magnification does not move consistently in one direction, and its mean sits closer to zero than any comparison this study reports except the one taken up in §5.4.

The second column records which of the two learned components — the convolutional stem and the transformer — each pair carries, because §5.3.4 shows this to bear on the direction of the effect.

### 5.3.3 The most consequential observation

The largest single movement in this table is not a gain. `config_2 → config_12` loses **−0.0541** pooled macro F1 when magnification is switched on, and `config_2` is not an arbitrary configuration: §5.1.2 establishes it as the best-performing configuration in the entire study, at a pooled macro F1 of 0.7122. Adding motion magnification to the best configuration found in this thesis makes it worse — by more than three and a half times the magnitude of magnification's own mean effect, and in the direction opposite to what that mean would suggest. This has to be stated plainly rather than folded into the aggregate: whatever magnification does on average, its effect on this study's headline configuration is a clear regression.

The largest gain sits at the opposite end of the same table. `config_9 → config_7` improves by **+0.0795**, the single biggest movement magnification produces in either direction, and this pair carries both a convolutional stem and a transformer — the two learned components this thesis ablates. The best and worst outcomes for magnification therefore occur in architecturally distinct settings: its worst result is a transformer with no spatial stem, its best is a transformer with one.

### 5.3.4 A pattern, not a conclusion

Four of the six pairs in Table 5.3 carry a convolutional stem (`config_9→7`, `config_6→8`, `config_3→13`, `config_5→16`), and three of those four improve with magnification; only `config_5→config_16` is negative, and only slightly so, at −0.0109. The two pairs without a stem (`config_1→4` and `config_2→12`) split one positive, one negative, with the stem-free negative case being magnification's single largest loss.

This is suggestive rather than conclusive, and it has to be reported as such. Three of four is not a large sample, and one of two is smaller still; §4.1.5 records that every configuration here was trained on a single fixed seed with no variance estimate across seeds, so neither the 3-of-4 split nor the 1-of-2 split carries any confidence interval. A repeat run under a different seed could shift either count. The pattern is worth naming because it is the only structure visible in an otherwise near-zero mean, but naming it is not the same as establishing it.

### 5.3.5 Interpretation

Two facts recorded in Chapters 2 and 3 bear directly on why magnification's mean effect sits so close to zero in this pipeline, and both are offered here as explanations consistent with the measurement, not as claims this study's design can test.

The first is a matter of pipeline ordering. §2.3.5 records that the calling code resamples each clip to a fixed frame count before invoking the magnifier, and passes the corpus's nominal 200 fps sampling rate to the magnifier's frame-rate parameter regardless of the resampled sequence's true frame spacing. The temporal band-pass filter that is meant to isolate motion in the 5–25 Hz range is therefore operating on an axis whose actual spacing depends on how much a given clip was subsampled — the realised pass-band is clip-dependent, not the fixed 5–25 Hz band the design intends. A filter whose frequency axis does not match the signal it is filtering is not obviously going to amplify the right motion consistently across clips of different lengths.

The second is a matter of what magnification is being placed in front of. §3.2.5 and §3.2.6 establish that every reviewed application of Eulerian magnification in the micro-expression literature evaluates it in front of an *appearance-based* representation — LBP, HOG, HIGO descriptors, or a deep encoder consuming pixel intensities directly. This thesis's pipeline instead feeds magnified frames into a dense optical-flow and optical-strain stage, which is itself an operator that recovers motion rather than reading appearance. Amplifying intensity change before a motion-estimation step is not the same operation as amplifying it before an appearance descriptor: the flow stage may partly absorb or distort whatever amplification precedes it, rather than passing an amplified signal through unchanged. Amplifying motion before computing motion is not obviously additive, and no experiment in this study isolates whether the ordering effect of §2.3.5 or this representational mismatch is the larger contributor to the near-zero mean — both are plausible, and neither is tested here.

### 5.3.6 Cost

Motion magnification is a data-pipeline operation, not a network component: §3.2.7 records it as a preprocessing step that selects which tensor directory a configuration reads from, and it adds no learnable parameters to any architecture in this study. Its cost is preprocessing time only, incurred once when the magnified tensor set is produced, not during training. The last-fold training-time and peak-VRAM figures recorded in §4.7.2 are training costs exclusively; they say nothing about the cost of magnification itself, and no configuration's stored cost figures include it. Set against a mean effect of +0.0152 that reverses sign on this study's best configuration, magnification's cost profile does not help decide whether the component is worth retaining — that question turns entirely on the effect reported above, not on any expense this section could offset it against.
