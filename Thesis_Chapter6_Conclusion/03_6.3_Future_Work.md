## 6.3 Further Work

§5.8 closes by asking what a stronger design would still need to establish. The answer falls into three kinds: changes that would make this study's numbers testable, experiments its own findings point at, and the backlog of departures §3.10.5 ledgers.

**Making the numbers testable.** Three changes repair the evaluation rather than the model, and none requires an architectural change. The first is an inner validation split. §4.6.2 records that checkpoint selection and final scoring use the same held-out fold, and §5.7.4 identifies this as the one respect in which the figures reported here are more favourable than the protocol supports. Selecting the checkpoint on subjects withheld from the training fold, and scoring the test subject once only, is the single change that would make every figure in Chapter 5 trustworthy, and demands no architectural change. The second is retaining per-clip predictions, which §4.6.6 records are not stored. Keeping them costs nothing and converts every comparison in Chapter 5 from a difference between point estimates into one a paired test — McNemar's, over the clips on which two configurations disagree — could adjudicate. The third, and the most generic, is repeating the sweep under several seeds. §4.1.5 fixes the design at a single seed and §5.2.3 states precisely what that costs: six same-direction matched pairs evidence a *sign*, not a magnitude. Multi-seed runs are what would attach an interval to a quantity this design can only order.

**What the findings point at.** Of the experiments the findings themselves motivate, §3.6.6 puts one first, and it is the clearest unforced departure from the literature: the stem arm re-trained at 28 × 28 and at 56 × 56, inside the range the reviewed work converged on, with everything else held as Chapter 4 specifies. It is the cheapest experiment left undone and the one whose outcome would most change how §5.5's result must be worded. Second, magnification should be applied before subsampling rather than after, so that the realised pass-band is the 5–25 Hz band §3.2.7 derives rather than a clip-dependent fraction of it (§3.5.7). Third, the three improvements §3.8.6 draws from its own divergence table, in that section's order: replacing mean pooling with an LSTM aggregator, the variant SLSTT's own ablation favours (§3.8.4); recomputing the input as long-term onset-referenced flow rather than consecutive-frame flow, a Stage 1 change costing no training time; and replacing the fixed sinusoidal encoding with learned position embeddings, at a negligible parameter cost. None of the three was tested here, and none disturbs the ablation's conclusions. Fourth, the stem itself: §5.5.5 sets out three candidate explanations for its behaviour — a spatial-only kernel, an input that already encodes motion analytically, and a resolution far above the literature's — and states that this study cannot adjudicate between them. Varying kernel shape, input resolution and the stem's position relative to the flow computation independently of one another is the design that would.

**The declared-divergence backlog.** Read forwards, the departures this review records are themselves a list of experiments. Two come from §3.3.7 rather than from the ledger: flow-based fine alignment, which that section names the most promising unexploited improvement and which this pipeline forgoes, and Farnebäck against TV-L1 as the estimator. From §3.10.5's twelve divergences come the dedicated strain post-filter this pipeline omits, and the four-term strain magnitude, whose √2 shear weighting the three-term form declared in §3.4.3 does not carry. The inherited preprocessing parameters are the other half of the backlog: sweeping T (§3.5.7), and sweeping α, λ and γ (§3.10.4), would establish whether the effects reported here survive away from the single point at which they were measured. A strain-on/strain-off arm, which §3.4.7 names the natural next ablation, would extend the matrix to the one input channel this design holds constant. Attention carries its own short list. §3.7.8 names three follow-ups, all cheap because the module trains no parameters: whether SimAM's distinctiveness prior suits feature maps derived from flow rather than from pixel intensity, what re-tuning λ would do, and the untested two-dimensional against three-dimensional pooling variant §3.7.7 decides without measuring. §3.7.7 also leaves retention itself open — the component is cheap rather than free (§5.4.5), and whether that price is worth an effect indistinguishable from noise is a question a larger evidence base would settle. Two further arms are cheap additions to the matrix rather than new machinery: focal loss against plain cross-entropy, and the values of γ and of label smoothing, all three fixed throughout this study (§3.9.8). §3.9 records two topics excluded from the implemented system that belong to any fuller account of this pipeline — Grad-CAM++ visual auditing, which would show whether the transformer attends where the action units are, and adversarial identity disentanglement, which bears directly on the single-demographic limitation §6.2 states.

Beyond the matrix, the corpus itself is the binding constraint. §3.1.5 states the cost of remaining single-corpus — that no claim here extends to other populations, other capture conditions or other corpora — and a composite-database evaluation of the kind §3.1.6 describes is the direct test of whether any effect reported here survives outside CASME II. One item, finally, is not an experiment but a safeguard: §3.2.7's verification helper should be wired in as a start-up assertion, so that a routing defect of the kind it was written to catch cannot recur silently.
